What is Cryptography?

Cryptography is the study and practice of techniques for secure communication in the presence of third parties called adversaries. It deals with developing and analyzing protocols that prevent malicious parties from retrieving information shared between two entities.

Data Confidentiality, Integrity, Authentication, and Non-repudiation are the four core principles of modern cryptography — often called the CIA+ triad.

alice_to_bob.py 
# Sender: Alice encrypts message
plaintext  = "Meet me at midnight"
key        = "SECRET_KEY_128BIT"
ciphertext = E(plaintext, key)
# → "3f9a21bc4d7e8f12..."

# Receiver: Bob decrypts
plaintext  = D(ciphertext, key)
# → "Meet me at midnight"
🔒

Confidentiality

Restricts information access to authorized parties only.

🛡️

Data Integrity

Ensures data remains accurate and unmodified over its lifecycle.

✅

Authentication

Verifies that the claimed identity of a party is genuine.

📜

Non-repudiation

Prevents a sender from denying they sent a message.

What is a Key?

In cryptography, a key is a string of characters used within an encryption algorithm to transform data so that it appears random. Like a physical key, it locks (encrypts) data so that only someone with the right key can unlock (decrypt) it.

Plaintext  +  Key  =  Ciphertext

The original data is known as the plaintext, and the data after the key encrypts it is the ciphertext.

Keys in Early Encryption — Caesar Cipher

Before computers, ciphertext was often created by replacing one letter with another, a method known as a substitution cipher. In Caesar Cipher, each letter is shifted by a fixed number of positions in the alphabet.

caesar_cipher_example 
# Shift = +3
Plaintext :  H E L L O W O R L D
Shift     :  +3 +3 +3 +3 +3 +3 +3 +3 +3 +3
Ciphertext:  K H O O R Z R U O G

Such ciphers are easy to break with statistical analysis — certain letters appear more often in any given language (E is most common in English). This led to the invention of the one-time pad — a single-use key at least as long as the plaintext, making each character shift unique.

one_time_pad_example 
# One-Time Pad key = [7, 17, 24, 9, 11]
Plaintext :  H  E  L  L  O
Key       :  +7 +17 +24 +9 +11
Ciphertext:  O  V  J  U  Z
